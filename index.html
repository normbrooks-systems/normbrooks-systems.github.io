I work across process, data, machinery, and people to find why systems underperform and build practical ways to make them work better. My background in operations and manufacturing combines hands-on process knowledge with root-cause analysis and practical implementation. I get close enough to understand the work, then step back to see what the process asks people to compensate for.

Selected Case Studies

01 Production Dashboard

What began as a two-week Google Sheets dashboard grew into a configurable production-management system spanning planning, shift initialization, production capture, downtime and setup tracking, pace and projections, historical analysis, and floor reporting based on the latest entered data. The architecture was adapted across departments and facilities, including the Planner and related support sheets I traveled to Iowa to help configure in December 2025. Its scope approached some Manufacturing Execution System (MES) functions. Later work on automated production capture and enterprise integration remained in development and did not reach production deployment.

02 FlowMaster Process Optimization

A personalized-mail process was being paced by a person manually opening finished envelopes to verify that the carrier matched the contents. The machine already had cameras, matching logic, and the ability to stop on a mismatch; it simply could not see the carrier's existing Data Matrix code. I improvised a simple camera mount that brought the carrier into the machine's match sequence. The change turned delayed manual inspection into immediate containment and released machines and roughly one to five people per shift for other work, depending on staffing. The capacity released helped eliminate mandatory overtime affecting six shifts.

03 Class 15 Reslot

At H.T. Hackney, I reorganized a 627-SKU tobacco department using historical movement, product relationships, walking distance, physical access, and practical picking considerations. In the surviving nine-week movement data, the areas assigned to cigars, snuff, and fast movers together accounted for roughly 95% of recorded activity. The redesign placed frequent activity within easier reach, while a central cut-through reduced unnecessary end-to-end travel. The result was a layout that was easier to navigate and required substantially less labor: work that had routinely needed multiple people could normally be handled by one.

Read the case study →

04 Inventory Control & Root-Cause Analysis

Routine stock-outs became an early lesson in root-cause analysis. Instead of automatically treating a missing item as shrink, I began tracing discrepancies through item histories, physical locations, system entries, and — when the value justified it — receiving paperwork. Over time I developed a simple value-based triage system that concentrated investigative effort where it was likely to matter. During that period, shrink remained below $1,000 against approximately $5 million in inventory across multiple inventory cycles.

Experience in Context

My career started in operations rather than engineering or analytics. At H.T. Hackney, the analytical work emerged gradually alongside the job: inventory forensics, systems documentation, rebuilding degraded forms, and eventually redesigning a department around how the work actually moved. The titles changed slowly; the scope of the problems I was willing to investigate kept expanding.

I later spent more than a decade in high-volume direct-mail production. Years on the floor gave me a detailed understanding of machinery, production flow, failure modes, staffing, and the difference between a process on paper and the same process during a real shift. I did not begin deliberately pursuing formal advancement until 2024. Once I did, I moved rapidly into supervision and then Production Analyst (actually titled Production Coordinator) work, with increasing emphasis on production analytics, internal tooling, process improvement, and cross-facility systems development.

In 2026, I chose not to jump immediately into another conventional role. I have used the year as an intentional reset: time to recenter, work on independent technical projects, and decide more deliberately where I want to apply the next stage of my career. The common thread through the earlier work remains the same — understand the real process, find the actual constraint, and make the system easier for people to use well.

Selected Passions

Photography

Photography is a longstanding interest of mine and another place where I tend to think in systems. Every image has constraints — light, time, movement, perspective, composition — and the interesting part is deciding which variables to control and which to let move.

Long exposures are a particular favorite. Imperfect Permanence uses one exposure to hold three different relationships with time in the same frame: a weathered structure, an ocean that never stops moving, and people present only long enough to leave traces.

Racing

Racing has been part of my background since childhood. My grandfather, Earl Brooks Sr., raced in NASCAR's top division, and I grew up around race-car fabrication. That history is personally meaningful, but it is background rather than a professional credential: my own work has to stand on its own.

What continues to interest me about racing is the system around the speed — machinery, operations, data, constraints, human decisions, and competitors reacting to one another in real time. I would be glad to apply the same systems thinking I use elsewhere to motorsports, but racing is one field of interest rather than the boundary of it.

Building & Experimentation

I like understanding technologies from the inside. My independent work has ranged across embedded computing, machine vision, automation, data systems, 3D printing, and mechanical projects. Not every experiment needs to become a product. Often the value is in learning enough of the system to understand what it can do, where it fails, and what I would change next.
